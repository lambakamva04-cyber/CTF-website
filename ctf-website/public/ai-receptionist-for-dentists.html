A dental practice loses money in a very specific way.

It is not the patient who phones and gets told to wait. It is the one who phones, hears a voicemail, and phones the next practice on the list instead. You never find out that call happened.

There are only a handful of moments where it goes wrong, and they are all the same moment: the phone rings and nobody can pick it up. Your receptionist is checking a patient in. She is on the other line. It is one o'clock. It is after five. It is Saturday. It is the week she is on leave.

A new patient is worth the whole course of treatment, not the one appointment. Which is why a phone that rings out is an expensive piece of equipment to own.

What it does for a practice

  • •Books new patients. It qualifies the call, offers real slots and takes the appointment. The patient is booked before they have a chance to phone anyone else.
  • •Reschedules without a callback. The single most common call a practice gets, handled at the moment it comes in.
  • •Catches cancellations early. A patient who cancels at eight in the evening gives you the whole next morning to refill the slot. A patient who cancels to a voicemail gives you nothing.
  • •Answers the boring questions. Where you are, where to park, which schemes you are contracted to, what your hours are over the holidays.
  • •Escalates real emergencies. It does not triage and it does not give clinical advice. It gives the caller the emergency route you have chosen and flags the call to you straight away.
  • •Takes the overflow. Ten patients can phone at the same time and all ten get answered.

It is not there to replace your receptionist.

Your receptionist is worth the most when she is with the patient standing in front of her, reading the room, handling the nervous one, sorting out an account query face to face. She is worth the least when she is apologising to a person on hold.

CTF takes the calls that arrive while she is already busy, and all the calls that arrive when the practice is shut. That is it. Practices that have tried to replace a front desk with software have learned an expensive lesson, and we are not going to sell you that.

Dental practices ask us this

What is an AI receptionist for a dental practice?

It is a voice that answers your practice phone, understands what the caller wants, and books, reschedules or cancels the appointment in your diary. It runs 24 hours a day and answers every call at once, so a patient never hears a busy tone or a voicemail beep.

Can it book, reschedule and cancel appointments?

Yes. Booking and rescheduling are the two things it is built to do well, because they are the two things that cost a practice money when nobody answers. Cancellation handling matters just as much: a cancellation you hear about on the night frees a slot you can still fill.

Will it handle a dental emergency correctly?

It does not give clinical advice and it does not try to triage. It recognises that the call is urgent, gives the caller the emergency route you have told us to give, and flags the call to you immediately rather than leaving it in a morning summary.

Does it work with medical aid questions?

It can answer the factual questions you have taught it, such as which schemes you are contracted to and whether you bill the scheme directly. It will not quote a patient a co-payment or interpret their benefits, because getting that wrong costs you a patient and a complaint.

Is patient information safe?

The receptionist only holds what it needs to book the appointment, and it only passes that to you. Under POPIA your practice is the responsible party, which means the obligation lands on you, not on your supplier. So ask every provider you evaluate, including us, exactly what is stored, where it is stored and for how long, and get the answer in writing before you sign anything.

Does it sound South African?

Yes. It is built around South African accents, South African trading hours and the way South African patients actually talk on the phone. Phone the demo line on this page and judge the voice yourself before you believe anything written here.

What does it cost a dental practice?

A once off build fee to configure and train the receptionist on your practice, then a monthly fee based on your call volume and how many locations you run. We quote on a fifteen minute call. If the maths does not work for your practice we will tell you on that call rather than sell you something.

Do I have to replace my receptionist?

No, and most practices should not. Your receptionist is at her best with the patient in front of her. CTF takes the calls that arrive while she is already on the phone, at lunch, or gone for the day.
